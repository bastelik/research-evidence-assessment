# Research, Evidence Assessment & Analytical Methodologies

A practical toolkit for turning complex information into transparent, traceable and decision-useful analysis.

It is designed for research, policy and program teams, consultants, editors, evaluators and analysts working with documents, interviews, reports, datasets and AI-generated claims.

## Analytical workflow

1. Define the decision or research question.
2. Establish scope, terms and inclusion criteria.
3. Gather relevant sources and record provenance.
4. Extract claims, findings, methods and limitations.
5. Assess source quality and evidentiary support.
6. Compare sources and investigate disagreement.
7. Separate observation, interpretation and recommendation.
8. Write findings with appropriate qualification.
9. Make limitations and unresolved questions visible.
10. Preserve an audit trail so another analyst can follow the reasoning.

## Evidence discipline

- A source's authority depends on the claim being assessed.
- A primary source is not automatically reliable for every interpretation.
- Correlation does not establish causation.
- Absence of evidence is not always evidence of absence.
- A statement not found in a source should not be presented as established by that source.
- Conflicting evidence should be described, not silently discarded.
- Recommendations should be linked to findings and assumptions.

## Responsible use

This toolkit is a general analytical aid. It does not replace formal research ethics review, legal advice, statistical expertise, domain-specific standards or independent verification when these are required. Protect personal, confidential and sensitive information throughout the research lifecycle.

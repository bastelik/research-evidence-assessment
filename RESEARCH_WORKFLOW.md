# Structured Research Workflow

## Stage 1 — Frame the question

Write one primary question and define:
- Purpose and intended decision
- Scope: subject, population, geography and period
- Key terms and operational definitions
- What is out of scope
- Required depth, format and deadline
- Ethical, privacy and access constraints

A broad topic is not yet a research question. Make the question answerable with available methods and evidence.

## Stage 2 — Plan the search

Identify likely source types:
- Primary documents and administrative records
- Peer-reviewed research and technical reports
- Official statistics and institutional publications
- Interviews, testimony and field observations
- Reputable secondary analysis

Create search terms, variants, languages and inclusion/exclusion criteria. Record where and when searches were conducted.

Keep the original source where permitted. Record page, section, table or timestamp for important findings.

## Stage 4 — Extract information

For each source, distinguish:
- What the source directly states
- What data or observation it presents
- The author's interpretation
- The source's limitations
- Your own analytical inference

Do not merge these layers in notes.

## Stage 5 — Assess and compare

Use the evidence assessment framework. Check scope, definitions, methodology, date, independence and corroboration. Search for contrary evidence, not only confirmation.

## Stage 6 — Analyze

Choose a method appropriate to the question: thematic synthesis, comparative analysis, chronology, descriptive statistics, document analysis or another justified approach. Explain how categories were defined and how exceptions were handled.

## Stage 7 — Draft findings

Organize around the research question rather than around a sequence of sources. For each finding:
1. State the conclusion.
2. Present the evidence.
3. Explain the reasoning.
4. Note relevant counterevidence or limitations.
5. State the degree of confidence.

## Stage 8 — Review

- Does every material claim have traceable support?
- Are quotations accurate and in context?
- Are dates, names, figures and units checked?
- Are facts separated from interpretation?
- Are alternative explanations considered?
- Are limitations and uncertainty visible?
- Does the conclusion answer the original question?

## Stage 9 — Deliver and preserve

Provide an executive summary, method, findings, limitations, references and appendices as appropriate. Retain a source log, analytical notes and version history consistent with privacy and retention requirements.
